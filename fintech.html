Fintech Credit Risk and Underwriting Optimization

MySQL and Power BI analysis of 39,717 historical LendingClub loans to find where default risk concentrates and what a stricter underwriting rule would cost.

39,717loans analysed
$445.6Mportfolio
$68.1Mcharged off
19.1%default rate, targeted segment
View repository

Business problem

Digital lenders must balance loan growth against credit risk. I set out to answer five questions that support underwriting decisions:

  1. Do larger loans show higher observed default rates?
  2. How does housing status relate to risk and exposure?
  3. How much loan amount is associated with Charged Off records?
  4. How do default rates differ by verification status?
  5. What is the historical trade-off of restricting renters who request $20,000 or more?

Approach

I profiled the data in MySQL first (record counts, duplicate IDs, missing values, categories), then grouped loans into tiers: Small up to $10,000, Medium up to $20,000 and High above that. Each question was answered with a documented query, and Charged Off was treated as the default outcome.

Key findings

Default rises with loan size

13.12% for small loans, about 14.36% for medium and about 18.76% for high.

Housing shows different risk and exposure

Mortgage borrowers have the lowest default rate (13.18%) but more than $224M of exposure. Renters default at 15.02% with a large share of volume.

About $68.11M is tied to charged-off loans

Roughly 15.3% of the total loan amount. This is not the same as final accounting loss, since recoveries and fees are not included.

Verified loans defaulted more, not less

16.01% for verified loans against 12.66% for not verified. This is an association, possibly caused by verification being applied to riskier applicants.

The renter segment asking for $20K+ is risky, but a ban has a cost

Its historical default rate is 19.14% (401 charged-off loans, about $9.88M). The same segment holds 1,571 fully paid and 123 current loans worth about $40.6M that a blanket ban would also block.

Recommendations

  • Apply stronger risk controls to larger loan requests.
  • Test targeted measures for the renter and $20K+ segment, such as risk-based limits, pricing or extra verification, before a blanket restriction.
  • Investigate why verified loans default more often.
  • Monitor both default rate and financial exposure, because they measure different risks.

Limitations

  • Historical, observational data: it shows associations, not causes.
  • Charged Off is a proxy for default, and the charged-off amount is not final accounting loss.
  • The underwriting scenario is a historical simulation, not a predictive model.
  • Current and Fully Paid amounts are not guaranteed future revenue.
