Traffic Accident Analysis

Business problem

A city transport agency wants to know which conditions are associated with more injuries and more serious crashes, so safety measures can be targeted well. Raw injury counts can mislead, because most driving happens in clear weather, daylight and on dry roads. So I combined counts with injury rates.

Data preparation

In MySQL I inspected the table, removed 31 duplicate records (209,306 down to 209,275), reviewed unknown values in key fields and prepared the date fields. The cleaning and analysis scripts are in the repository.

Questions

  1. Which weather condition has the highest total injuries?
  2. Which lighting condition has the highest and lowest incapacitating injuries?
  3. Which road surface has the most crashes, and what is its most common cause?
  4. What is the most common first crash type?
  5. When does the most common crash type peak and dip?
  6. Which weather condition has the highest injury rate per 100 crashes?
  7. Which lighting condition has the highest incapacitating rate per 1,000 crashes?
  8. What share of dry-road crashes have no determined cause?

Key findings

Clear weather has the most injuries, but rain has the highest rate

Clear weather recorded 64,288 injuries, mostly because about 79% of crashes happen in clear weather. Per 100 crashes, rain is highest at 41.2, against 39.0 for clear weather and about 38 overall. The gap is modest, so rain is a signal to investigate, not proof of a large effect.

Lighted darkness has the highest incapacitating rate

Darkness on a lighted road records 49.3 incapacitating injuries per 1,000 crashes, against 34.0 in daylight. Daylight has the most incapacitating injuries (4,557) only because it has the most crashes.

A quarter of dry-road crashes have no determined cause

Dry roads have the most crashes, and 41,610 of them (26.7%) are recorded as Unable to determine. That is a data-quality gap, not a cause.

Turning is the most common first crash type

About 64,150 crashes, or 30.7% of the total.

Monthly pattern

The most common crash type, No injury or drive away, peaks in October (11,136) and dips in April (8,463). Month length and the years covered affect these counts, and 2025 is a partial year.

Recommendations

  1. Prioritise safety measures where risk per crash is highest, such as rain and lighted darkness, and not only where crashes are most numerous.
  2. Improve how crash causes are recorded.
  3. Investigate turning crashes, especially at intersections.

Limitations

  • Observational data: it shows associations, not causes.
  • No traffic-volume figures, so rates per vehicle or per mile cannot be calculated.
  • 2013 and 2025 are partial years, so annual and monthly comparisons may be affected.
  • Small categories such as Unknown lighting should not be over-interpreted.
